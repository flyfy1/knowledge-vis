## The one question

你账户里的那个数字不是金库里的一叠钞票。它是什么，是谁造出来的，为什么同样的数字每年能买的东西都少一点？
Ch1 answers *what* (an IOU), ch2–3 *who made it and why it is safe* (bank loans, runs, insurance), ch4–5 *why it buys less and who pays for that*, ch6–8 *what borrowing that money costs and how the central bank moves that price*, ch9 *what it buys abroad*, ch10 what the reader can now work out.

## Chapters

Each: law · everyday case · model (state; parameters; reader controls; what to discover; exact solution) · limits.

**1 钱是一张人人都收的欠条 (#ch1).** Law: money is an IOU; whoever's IOU everyone accepts issues money. Case: baker wants fish, fisher wants shoes, shoemaker wants bread. Model `trade*`: 48 people, N kinds of goods (person i makes i mod N, wants a seeded other kind); every round a random perfect matching; three economies share people, pairings and coin flips: barter (needs a double coincidence and both still looking), personal IOU (seller accepts with probability q), bank IOU (q = 1). Controls N (2–12), q (10–100%), restart, +10 rounds. Steps at 4 rounds/s on dt for 60 rounds; reduced motion jumps to round 60. Discover: barter stalls (at N = 8, 18 of 48 people have no double coincidence anywhere), personal IOUs at q = 10% are no better than barter for 30 rounds, at q = 100% identical to the bank's. Exact: expected rounds for an IOU buyer = mean over people of (P−1)/(sellers of the wanted kind × q) → 7.8 (q = 1), 15.7 (q = 0.5) at N = 8. Limits: says why IOUs work, not how money arose historically (contested: the barter-first textbook story vs. credit-first accounts, e.g. Graeber 2011); no default, forgery or storage costs.

**2 贷款凭空造出存款 (#ch2).** Law: a loan writes a new deposit. Case: a 10万 car loan. Model `bank*`: one balance sheet for the whole banking system; R reserves, L loans, D deposits, B central-bank borrowing, E equity = 10. Start R 10, L 90, D 90 (one loan just made, outlined). Loan: L += 10, D += 10, refused if E/(L+10) < k (capital) or, in "textbook" mode, if R < rr·(D+10); in "today" mode the shortfall is borrowed from the central bank (B). Repay deletes 10 of each (kept ≥ one loan). "QE" adds 10 to R and D (central bank buys a bond from a household). Controls: mode, k (4–12%), rr (0–20%), five buttons; deposits history chart with the textbook ceiling R/rr. Discover: textbook stops after 1 loan on reserves (D = 100 = R/rr); today's description stops after 3 on capital, borrowing 2; after QE the textbook ceiling is 200 but capital stops D at 130; rr = 0 never binds. Limits: one bank (single banks also lose reserves when payments leave them); loan demand not modelled; risk weights all 100%. Contested claims carry tags in the page: reserves not a binding constraint where the central bank targets a rate with ample reserves (较有把握); how much the RRR still binds where it is actively used, e.g. China (一半一半).

**3 银行没亏钱，也会倒 (#ch3).** Law: maturity mismatch lets a solvent bank fail when enough withdraw. Case: hearing that everyone else is withdrawing. Model `run*` (Diamond–Dybvig style): 100 depositors of 1; 20 impatient; bank keeps λ in cash, lends 1−λ at gross return R = 1.5, early sale fetches s per unit; paid 1 each, first come first served. Stayers' payout V(f) = ((λ−f) + (1−λ)R)/(1−f) for f ≤ λ, else (1−λ−(f−λ)/s)R/(1−f), 0 once the loans are gone. Threshold f* (V = 1) = (R(1−λ) + Rλ/s − 1)/(R/s − 1). Patient depositor j has pessimism e_j evenly on (0, 0.15) and withdraws when f + e_j > f*; a rumour sends the most pessimistic share ρ at once; rounds repeat until no one else moves. Insurance: stayers are guaranteed 1, so no cascade; insurer pays any shortfall. Controls ρ (0–50%, one person per step), λ (10–60%), s (0.3–1.0), insurance, replay; rounds revealed every 0.7 s. Defaults λ 0.3, s 0.5, ρ 25% → f* 47.5%, V(0.2) = 1.44, smallest rumour that tips it = 16 people, run pays the first 65 and leaves 35 with nothing; insured: no cascade, insurer pays 0; s = 1: no threshold. Limits: pessimism spread is assumed; no distinction between insolvency and illiquidity beyond the model; moral hazard of insurance mentioned only.

**4 钱多了，东西就贵吗 (#ch4).** Law: long run, money outgrowing goods lowers each unit's value; velocity sits in between. Case: MV = PY as an identity, then the two assumptions needed to make it causal. Model `qtm*`, yearly in logs: n = m + v, potential grows 2%; p_t = p_{t−1} + π_{t−1} + θ(n − y* − p_{t−1} − π_{t−1}), θ = 0.35 (sticky prices + adaptive expectations); y = n − p. Supply shock = one-off 8% cost push into prices, money unchanged. Six years of history at 2% money growth (prices still), then the reader's money growth (0–16%) and velocity change (−5..+5%) from year 1; buttons shock / restart / finish; one year per 0.5 s. Default 8%: output +4.9% in year 2, inflation 8.0% in year 4 (overshoot), output below normal years 5–9, settles at 5.9% = 1.08/1.02 − 1. Velocity −3% → 2.7%. Shock: inflation up and output down together, then the price level drifts back. Exact long run: (1+gM)(1+gV)/(1+gy) − 1. Limits: one sector, no interest rate, no central-bank reaction, backward-looking expectations. Contested (tags on page): high/persistent inflation is monetary (较有把握); supply shocks raise prices short-run (较有把握); money as a predictor at low inflation (一半一半); shares of money, fiscal and supply in 2021–22 (只是猜测).

**5 通胀从谁手里拿走了钱 (#ch5).** Law: inflation moves wealth; surprise decides how much. Case: a fixed debt. Model `winPath`: contract at year 0 with expected e; i = 1.02(1+e) − 1; prices rise at a in years 1–4, then e; wage indexed to inflation `lag` years earlier (e before year 1). Real values in year-0 money over 10 years. Controls a (0–20%), e (0–10%), lag (0–3), import ch4. Default e 2%, a 8%, lag 1: i 4.04%, saver gets 97.0 vs plan 121.9 → 24.9 to the borrower; real wage −5.6% for four years ≈ 2.7 months' pay (lag 2: 5.2). e = a: i 10.16%, no transfer. Limits: no renegotiation, taxes or variable rates; wages ignore labour-market tightness.

**6 利息是时间的价格 (#ch6).** No model. Fisher relation using ch5's 4.04%; PV at 4% (104 in a year = 100; 100 in ten years = 67.6, `pv`); why old bonds fall; the central bank sets only the overnight rate. Links to compound-interest#step3 and interest-rates-bonds#step3.

**7 一笔房贷是怎样还掉的 (#ch7).** Law: each payment pays interest first. Model `amortise`: M = P·i/(1−(1+i)^−n), i = r/12; monthly loop; extra goes to principal; last payment deflated by inflation. P = 1,000,000 fixed; controls rate (1–8%), term (10–30), extra (0–3000/month), inflation (0–8%), import ch5. Default 4%, 30 y: M 4774; first month 69.8% interest; first five years 66.7%; total interest 71.9万; +1000/month → 21.6 years, 8.4 years sooner, 22.5万 less interest; at 2% inflation the last payment is worth 2640 today. Limits: fixed rate for 30 years; no fees, penalties or liquidity needs; explicitly not advice on prepaying.

**8 央行的利率怎样走到你的房贷 (#ch8).** Law: one rate, one to three years to reach prices. Model `transmit`, monthly first-order lags (10 sub-steps): policy d for 18 months then 0; market rate τ 1; new-loan rate τ 3; paid rate = v·new + (1−v)·fixed average, fixed resets 1/(12F) a month; spending gap → −(½ paid + ½ new) τ 6; inflation gap → 0.5 × spending τ 12. Controls d (0.25–4), v (0–100%), F (1–10 years), replay; cursor 4 months/s over 48 with the full path faint ahead. Default d 2, v 30%, F 5: spending trough month 19, inflation trough month 25 at −0.43 pp; v 100% → −0.60 and a month earlier; v 0 → −0.35. Labelled 一半一半 on timing; all coefficients illustrative. Limits: mortgages are the only channel (no exchange rate, asset prices, investment, expectations).

**9 同一个汉堡，不同的价钱 (#ch9).** Law: long run toward parity, slowly; short run capital flows. Model `fxAt`: burger 20 yuan at home, 4 B abroad → PPP 5; PPP moves with (1+πH)/(1+2%); real deviation q starts at ln 1.3 and halves every H years; shocks ±ln 1.2 at the current time (at year 8 under reduced motion or after the run ends). Controls πH (0–12%), H (1–8), two shocks, import ch4, replay; 1.5 years/s over 20. Default πH 5%, H 4: PPP 8.93 after 20 years; within 5% of parity after 9.75 years. Limits: one traded good with constant decay; Balassa–Samuelson (non-traded rent and wages) named; short-run direction labelled 一半一半 (Meese–Rogoff random-walk result).

**10 现在你能自己算的事 (#ch10).** Six things the reader can now check (QE news, a bank's risk, long-run inflation, an amortisation table, rate rises, cross-country prices), a three-question method, the one not-advice statement, and four links.
