## Energy enters from sunlight: producers catch only a small part {#step0 h2=s0t pre=1}

::: strings
nav0: 0 Sunlight in
:::

::: html step0-1
:::

This step answers: how much energy is there at the start of a food chain? Green plants (the **producers**) use photosynthesis to fix a small part of the sunlight as organic matter, and this is the entire energy source of the food chain. Say each square metre receives 5,000,000 kJ of solar energy a year and producers fix a fraction η (commonly about 1%); the fixed energy is 5,000,000 × η. In the figure the long bar is the incoming sunlight and the short bar is the energy fixed; most of the rest is reflected, turned into heat or unused. {#s0e .explain}

lEta: Efficiency η of photosynthesis in turning sunlight into organic matter (%)
:::

## Shrinking by level: each step up keeps only a small part {#step1 h2=s1t pre=1}

::: strings
nav1: 1 Shrinking by level
:::

::: html step1-1
:::

This step answers: as energy moves up the food chain, how much is left at each level? The trophic levels are producers (plants), primary consumers (herbivores), secondary consumers (carnivores that eat herbivores), tertiary consumers and so on. Of the energy a level eats from the one below, only part becomes its own body matter, the **transfer efficiency** (commonly about 10%); most of the rest is lost as heat by respiration, and some becomes uneaten remains and waste that goes to decomposers. With 1% of 5,000,000 kJ as the producers' starting point, level k has producers' energy × efficiency^(k−1). The bar widths below are proportional to energy. {#s1e .explain}

::: html step1-2

## How much room at the top? The higher the level, the more area one animal needs {#step2 h2=s2t pre=1}

::: strings
nav2: 2 Room at the top
:::

::: html step2-1
:::

This step answers: why are top predators so few? Suppose a medium-sized carnivore needs 365,000 kJ of energy a year (about a fox). Level k has only E of energy per square metre per year, so the area to keep one animal = 365,000 ÷ E. The higher the level, the smaller E and the larger the area; at some level the area is so large that a territory holds very few animals, or not even one, which is why food chains seldom exceed 4–5 levels. Producer energy here is 50,000 kJ/m²/yr (η = 1%). {#s2e .explain}

::: html step2-2
s2o: Move the trophic level from 1 to 6: watch the area needed (shown as squares, each a hectare = 10,000 m²) grow explosively; raise the transfer efficiency to 25% and see how much smaller the area at the same level becomes.
:::

## Three pyramids: energy is always upright, numbers and biomass can be inverted {#step3 h2=s3t pre=1}

::: strings
nav3: 3 Three pyramids
:::

::: html step3-1
:::

This step answers: can a pyramid turn upside down? The **pyramid of energy** (energy flowing per year) is always narrow at the top because thermodynamics forbids energy growing as it passes on. But the **pyramid of numbers** (individuals) and the **pyramid of biomass** (mass of living matter at one moment) can be inverted: thousands of insects live on one tree (inverted numbers); in the sea, phytoplankton are small and turn over very fast, so at one moment their biomass can be below that of the zooplankton that eat them (inverted biomass). Annual production = standing biomass × turnover (how many times it is replaced per year). Pick an ecosystem and compare the three pyramids (illustrative data, widths on a logarithmic scale). {#s3e .explain}

:::

## What you eat decides how many people can be fed: every extra level loses a lot {#step4 h2=s4t pre=1}

::: strings
nav4: 4 Diet and people fed
:::

::: html step4-1
:::

This step answers: why does a plant-based diet feed more people from the same land? A person needs about 3,800,000 kJ a year (about 2500 kcal a day). A hectare of cropland yields about 60,000,000 kJ of edible energy a year. Eating plants directly takes one unit of land per unit of energy; eating meat, the animals turn only a fraction of their feed energy (the conversion efficiency) into meat, so much more feed must be grown for the same energy. Land needed = energy required × [(1 − meat share) + meat share ÷ conversion efficiency] ÷ yield. Each dot in the figure is a person, the number fed by 10 hectares of cropland. {#s4e .explain}

:::

## Recap and self-check {#step5 h2=s5t pre=1}

::: strings
nav5: 5 Recap & check
:::

::: html step5-1
:::

**Recap**: (1) producers fix only about 1% of the incoming sunlight; (2) each step up keeps about 10%, so level k = producers × efficiency^(k−1); (3) the higher the level, the more area one animal needs, which is why food chains are short and top predators rare; (4) the energy pyramid is always narrow at the top while numbers and biomass pyramids can be inverted; (5) the nearer to the start of the food chain you take your energy, the more people the same land feeds. {#s5e .explain}
