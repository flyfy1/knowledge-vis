## The one question

The body keeps dozens of quantities within narrow ranges while everything going in and out keeps changing. How does it do that, and what happens when you push on it with a meal, a drink, a pill or a fever?

Answer the essay builds: three parts. (1) Inflow = outflow, with outflow proportional to the stock, gives a set point for free (ch1–2). (2) The shape of the exit decides whether there is a plateau at all: a capped exit has none (ch3 alcohol, ch5 heat stroke). (3) Feedback pulls a pushed quantity back, and its gain and delay decide whether it lags or overshoots (ch4 glucose, ch5 fever, ch7 heart). Windows sit on top: between useful and harmful (ch6), between selecting and killing (ch8), and a population threshold (ch9). Ch10 turns these into three questions to ask about any new push.

## Changes from the planning list

- **Alcohol (ch3) is framed as "an exit with a cap"** and the cap is reused in ch5 for heat stroke (hyperthermia), which gave the fever chapter its contrast (set point moved vs exit saturated). That reuse was not in the plan.
- **Therapeutic window (ch6) moved after fever** and reuses the ch2 dosing model with a "slower clearance" slider, because the most useful non-obvious result is that a slow clearer crosses the upper edge a day or more after starting.
- **Antibiotics (ch8)** reuse the ch2 dosing model for the concentration. The model turned out to show that "stopping early" favours resistance only at intermediate doses; at high doses an early stop lets *sensitive* bacteria back. I kept that, and the prose labels the "complete the course" debate with confidence tags instead of repeating the rule.
- **Heart (ch7)**: the law is "to hold one quantity steady, another must swing" (O₂ delivery held, heart rate moved), which ties it to the essay's question rather than being a cardiac-output fact sheet. Links to blood-circulation steps 3 and 4.
- **Herd immunity (ch9)** links to public-health step 0 for R₀ and only derives the threshold 1 − 1/R₀, framed as ch1's in/out balance at population scale.
- **Ending (ch10)** is three questions plus four things the reader can now reason about, not advice.
- The PID page is at `engineering/control/pid-control/` (not `signal/control/`); linked to its step 1.

## Chapters

Each: law · everyday case · model (state, parameters, reader controls, what to discover, closed form) · limits.

1. **漏水的桶 / Leaky bucket** (`#ch1`). Law: outflow ∝ stock ⇒ the stock finds its own stopping point. Case: bucket with a hole. Model: `dL/dt = I − kL`, exact step `L* + (L − L*)e^{−kh}`; reader owns I (0.5–3 L/min), k (10–50 %/min), ±5 L pushes; live sim, 3 model-min per s. Discover: L* = I/k (10 L by default), gap halves every ln2/k (3.5 min) whatever the push. Limits: one compartment, one exit.
2. **半衰期和平台 / Half-life and plateau** (`#ch2`). Law: repeated dosing climbs to a plateau in ~5 half-lives regardless of dose. Model: one compartment, instantaneous absorption, exact superposition `C(t) = Σ amt·2^{−(t−tᵢ)/t½}`; plateau peak `1/(1 − 2^{−τ/t½})`, trough = peak − 1, peak/trough = `2^{τ/t½}`. Reader: t½ 2–24 h, τ 4–24 h, plan (on time / miss the 48 h dose / miss then double). Discover: 97 % after 5 t½; τ = 3t½ gives peak/trough 8; double-up peak 2.49 (+25 %), missed trough 0.49. Replay clock 8 h/s. Limits: no absorption phase, fixed clearance.
3. **酒精 / Alcohol** (`#ch3`). Law: saturated enzyme ⇒ fixed amount per hour ⇒ no plateau if intake is faster. Model: Michaelis–Menten `Vmax·C/(Km + C)`, Vmax 15 mg/100 mL/h, Km 5; each drink 20 mg/100 mL into a gut compartment absorbed at 3/h (illustrative). Reader: drinks 1–8, spacing 15–120 min. Discover: near-straight fall (13.8/h at 60, 12.0/h at 20); each extra drink adds ~1.5 h (4 → 7.3 h, 8 → 13.2 h); cap 0.75 drinks/h, faster drinking climbs throughout. Limits: absorption, individual variation; not for estimating one's own BAC.
4. **血糖 / Blood glucose** (`#ch4`). Law: negative feedback; weak ⇒ stays high, late + strong ⇒ overshoot. Model (illustrative, in the spirit of Bergman's minimal model): gut `Q' = −Q/30`; insulin signal `I' = (g·max(0, G(t−d) − 5) − I)/12`; `G' = Ra − I·G/5 − 0.002(G − 5) + glucagon·0.06·max(0, 5 − G)`, Euler h = 0.25 min. Reader: insulin strength 10–200 %, delay 0–40 min, meal size, glucagon on/off. Discover: default peak 7.7 at ~27 min, 5.0 at 2 h; 10 % ⇒ peak 8.8, 7.6 at 2 h, ~90 min above 7.8; 200 % + 40 min ⇒ 9.0 then 2.8; without glucagon it stays down. Link: PID step 1. Limits: not fitted, no incretins/glycogen/exercise; not diagnostic.
5. **发烧 / Fever** (`#ch5`). Law: fever is a moved set point, defended. Model: `dT/dt = u + load − ice − 0.01(T − 37)`, `u = clamp(0.1(Tset − T), −0.06, +0.04)`; Tset relaxes to its target with τ = 20 min; steady states solved by bisection. Reader: infection (target 39), antipyretic (target back to 37), ice, external heat load 0–10. Discover: feels cold while already at 38; fever settles at 38.8; ice only reaches 38.0 with heat production maxed; antipyretic ⇒ sweating; load 6 ⇒ 37.5, 8 ⇒ 39.0, 10 ⇒ 41.0 with sweating maxed (hyperthermia, same structure as ch3's cap). Limits: single temperature, set-point concept itself debated (Romanovsky 2004), rates illustrative.
6. **治疗窗 / Therapeutic window** (`#ch6`). Law: dosing aims to keep the level between useful and harmful. Model: quantal Hill curves effect `1/(1+(0.8/C)³)`, harm `1/(1+(5/C)⁴)`; window = [EC50 = 0.8, TC5 = 2.4]; TI = 6.25; time course from the ch2 model, 3 doses/day total, t½ = 8 h × slowdown, 6 days. Reader: split 1–4×/day, slowdown ×1–×3. Discover: once daily ⇒ 3.4/0.4, 53 % in / 30 % below / 17 % above (time shares by the midpoint rule on a 0.01 h grid over [120 h, 144 h); equals the closed form 52.7 / 30.0 / 17.3); 3× ⇒ 2.0/1.0 all inside; slowdown ×2 ⇒ first crosses at 24 h, plateau 2.4–3.4 all above. Limits: invented curves, no effect lag, interactions or cumulative toxicity.
7. **心脏的预算 / The heart's budget** (`#ch7`). Law: to hold one quantity, another swings. Model: demand `5 + 2·level` L/min; SV rises linearly to its max by level 5 (untrained 70→130 mL, trained 90→170 mL, trained values illustrative); HR target = demand/SV capped at `208 − 0.7·age`; HR follows with τ = 20 s (illustrative); live sim at 3× real time. Reader: level 0–10, age 20–70, fitness. Discover: lag after a step; rest HR 71 vs 56, level 6 131 vs 100; HRmax 194 at 20, 166 at 60; at 60 untrained max CO 21.6 < 25 needed (short 3.4). Links: blood-circulation steps 3–4. Limits: no filling-time drop, blood pressure, redistribution, lungs.
8. **抗生素 / Antibiotics** (`#ch8`). Law: concentration selects; the band between MICs keeps resistant cells. Model: S and R strains, logistic growth r = 0.4/h, K = 10⁹; kill `0.6·C²/(MIC² + C²)` with MIC_S = 1, MIC_R = 8; immune `0.1·10³/(10³ + N)`; C from the ch2 model (every 8 h, t½ 4 h); a strain below 1 cell is gone; start 10⁸ S, 10² R. Reader: dose per administration (0–48 × MIC_S), course 1–10 days. Discover: 4× ⇒ R overtakes on day 1.3, S gone day 3.3, ends resistant; 24× ⇒ cleared day 3.3; 16× ⇒ R overtakes day 2.6, cleared day 6.4 if continued, resistant if stopped at day 6; 24× for 3 days ⇒ sensitive relapse. Prose labels: 较有把握 (sub-MPC concentrations select resistance), 有争议 (Llewelyn 2017 BMJ vs critics), 本页不回答 (course length for a real infection). Limits: one site, no commensals, no gene transfer, invented parameters.
9. **群体免疫 / Herd immunity** (`#ch9`). Law: Re < 1 ⇒ the outbreak dies; immunity pushes Re below 1. Model: Reed–Frost chain binomial on N = 1200, three index cases, seeded RNG, one generation per 0.6 s; deterministic final size `z = (1 − v)(1 − e^{−R₀z})`; threshold `1 − 1/R₀`. Reader: R₀ 1–18, coverage 0–99 %, reseed. Discover: R₀ 3, v 0.5 ⇒ Re 1.5, ~29 % infected; threshold 67 %; near the threshold outcomes split by luck; measles 12–18 ⇒ 92–94 %. Link: public-health step 0. Limits: homogeneous mixing, perfect lasting immunity.
10. **推它之前，先问三个问题 / Three questions** (`#ch10`). No model. How does it leave? Who corrects it, how fast and how hard? Where is the window? Then four things the reader can now reason about, a one-paragraph synthesis and 2–4 related links.

Reuse of earlier models by name: ch2 (bucket), ch3 (bucket with a capped hole), ch5 (ch3's cap), ch6 (ch2 model), ch7 (ch4 structure), ch8 (ch2 model), ch9 (ch1 balance).
