## Steps
**0 Private market.** Knows: curves. Question: where does a private market settle? Inputs a (60–120), c (0–60); seek Q=(a−c)/(b+d), CS, PS. b=d=1.
**1 External cost.** Knows: step 0. Question: social vs private cost? Input e (0–40); seek Q_m, Q*=(a−c−e)/(b+d), damage e·Q_m.
**2 Deadweight loss.** Knows: step 1. Question: how much is lost? Inputs e, supply slope d (0.3–3.0); seek ½e(Q_m−Q*) with Q_m−Q* = e/(b+d).
**3 Pigouvian tax.** Knows: DWL. Question: best tax? Inputs e, t (0–60); seek W(t) = (a−c)Q − ½(b+d)Q² − eQ, peak at t=e.
**4 Commons.** Knows: private vs social. Question: why overuse? Input m herders (1–20); seek N = 80m/(m+1), profit N(80−N). Holds: symmetric Cournot herders, revenue 100−N per cow, cost 20.
**5 Bargaining.** Knows: all. Question: can parties fix it? Inputs right holder, G, D, T; seek outcome and payment (midpoint split) when |G−D|>T. Links: supply-demand#step3, game-theory#step2, climate-feedback#step0.

## First-appearance terms

## Simulation vs reality
Constant marginal damage, linear curves, symmetric players, no strategic regulator; midpoint bargaining is one of many splits; real damages are uncertain.
