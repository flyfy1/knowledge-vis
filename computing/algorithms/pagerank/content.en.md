Step 0 · Who matters {#s0k .step-kicker i18n=text}

This step answers: how do we judge that a web page is important? The simplest way is to count **in-links** (the number of links from other pages pointing to it). The graph below has 8 pages A, B, C, X, Y, D, E and F; an arrow means “this page links to that page” and node size = in-links + 1. First pick the page you think is most important, then press “Reveal PageRank”: node size switches to PageRank (explained in later steps; for now think of it as “the probability that a random web surfer ends up on this page”, summing to 100% over the 8 pages). Look at the ranks of X (3 in-links, but the pages linking to it have no in-links themselves) and Y (only 1 in-link, but from an important page). {#s0e .explain}

## Click links at random: where do you end up? {#step1 h2=s1t}

::: strings
nav1: 1 Random surfer
:::

Step 1 · The random surfer {#s1k .step-kicker i18n=text}

This step answers: how can “important” become a computable number? Imagine a random surfer: at each step they follow a random outgoing link of the current page with probability d (the **damping factor**), and with probability 1 − d they get bored and jump to any of the 8 pages at random (**teleport**). The long-run fraction of time spent on a page is that page’s PageRank. Press “1 step / 100 steps / 1000 steps” or “Auto surf”: the coloured bars are the frequencies of visits so far and the white ticks are the exact PageRank; the more steps, the closer the bars get to the ticks. Dragging d clears the steps taken (the page tells you). {#s1e .explain}

::: html step1-1

Total variation distance = ½Σ|frequency − PageRank|; 0 means the two distributions are identical; more steps means less random fluctuation. {#s1term .term i18n=text}

## Without actually surfing, how do we compute it directly? {#step2 h2=s2t}

::: strings
nav2: 2 Iterating
:::

Step 2 · Iterating {#s2k .step-kicker i18n=text}

This step answers: can we compute each page’s PageRank directly, without simulation? Idea: start with every page equal (1/8), then update repeatedly — each page’s new score = (1 − d)/8 from teleporting, plus d times the score of every page linking to it, split evenly among that page’s outgoing links. This is **power iteration**, repeated multiplication by a matrix (see [[eigenvectors#step3|Eigenvectors · power iteration]]). Drag “iterations k” or press “Iterate step by step”: watch the bars drift from equal toward the white ticks (the final values), and the “change since last iteration” and “distance from the final values” in the readout shrink. Here d = 0.85. {#s2e .explain}

::: html step2-1

s2reset: Reset to 0 iterations
:::

The scores always sum to 1 after each iteration; smaller and smaller changes mean it is converging. {#s2term .term i18n=text}

## What does the damping factor d change? {#step3 h2=s3t}

::: strings
nav3: 3 Damping

Step 3 · The damping factor {#s3k .step-kicker i18n=text}

This step answers: how does PageRank change with d? The larger d, the more faithfully the surfer follows links and the more the result depends on link structure; the smaller d, the more time goes into random teleports and the more even the pages become. Drag d with the links fixed: see how node sizes, bars (white tick = the value at d = 0.85) and the ranking change; the curve below is the number of iterations needed to get the error below 10⁻⁸ and the orange dot is the current d: the closer d is to 1, the slower. The usual choice is d = 0.85. {#s3e .explain}

Step 4 · Add one link {#s4k .step-kicker i18n=text}

This step answers: which new link lifts a page’s rank noticeably? Tick the new links below (the thick white arrows in the graph): the bars are the new PageRank and the white ticks the old values. Observe: a link from an important page (such as A) matters far more than links from tiny pages nobody links to (D, E, F); and the more outgoing links a page splits its score among, the less each link passes on. ## Wrap-up: important = linked to by important pages {#step5 h2=s5t}

::: strings
nav5: 5 Finale
:::

Step 5 · Finale {#s5k .step-kicker i18n=text}

PageRank defines “important” as the long-run probability of a random surfer being on a page: it can be simulated or computed by iteration; many in-links do not guarantee importance, and a link from an important page weighs more. {#s5e .explain i18n=text}

::: html step5-1
c1t: In-link counts can mislead
c1b: X has more in-links, but Y’s in-link comes from an important page and ranks higher (step 0).
c2t: The random surfer
c2b: Follow links with the occasional teleport; long-run visit frequency is PageRank (step 1).
c3t: Solving by iteration
c3b: Spread scores along links repeatedly and it converges in tens of rounds; larger d is slower (steps 2 and 3).
c4t: Link quality
c4b: One link from an important page beats a crowd of tiny pages (step 4).
modelT: Model and limits
modelB: This is a toy network of 8 pages with no page lacking outgoing links (the code spreads such a page’s score evenly, but none appears in the graph); real search ranking uses many signals and PageRank was only an early one; real web graphs are enormous and need spam defences. Step 1 is a simulation with a fixed random seed; the other steps use exact iteration.
:::
