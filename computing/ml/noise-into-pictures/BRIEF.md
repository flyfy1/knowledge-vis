## The one question

How does a machine turn a cloud of random noise into a picture? Every image, video and audio generator of 2022–2026 in the diffusion / flow family does the same thing: start from an easy distribution (Gaussian noise) and follow a learned direction field to a distribution nobody can sample directly. The page runs every step of that argument on 2-D points, where everything is cheap and many optima are exact.

## Toy data and the yardstick (shared by all chapters)

- Spiral: 24 Gaussian blobs (sd 0.07) along a spiral, r from 0.3 to 1.5. Two moons (ch7): 12 blobs per class, sd 0.25, so the classes overlap. Song & Ermon's two-Gaussian mixture (ch3).
- Because the data are Gaussian mixtures, the noised density, its score, the posterior mean and the optimal flow-matching velocity are closed-form. The page shows the exact optimum next to a trained network.
- Distance: sliced Wasserstein-1 over 64 directions, 1,000 generated points against quantiles of a fixed 40,000-point true sample. The "floor" (0.024) is the mean score of 1,000 genuine samples over 8 seeds. "Indistinguishable" = within 1.5 × floor (a page-chosen threshold, said so in the text).
- Networks: MLP 10 → 48 → 48 → 2, SiLU; inputs = (x, y) and 8 sin/cos time features; Adam, cosine LR 3e-3 → 5e-5, 60,000 steps × 256 (train.cjs, ~74 s per net). Backprop by hand, gradient-checked in the test.

## Chapters

1. **Generation is sampling** (#ch1). Law: generating = drawing from p(x). Model: rejection sampling on the spiral inside a 4×4 box, animated (250 proposals/s); exact acceptance 1/(max p · area) = 3.4%. A Gaussian blob (σ 0.25 in [−1,1]^d) has acceptance (√(2π)σ/2)^d exactly: 9.8% in 2-D, ×0.31 per dimension, 10⁻³⁹⁵ at 784, 10⁻³⁹⁶³⁶² at 786,432. Reader: dimension slider. Limit: rejection is the naive method; the point is the curse of dimension.
2. **Destroying data is easy** (#ch2). DDPM forward process, T = 1000, β linear 1e-4 → 0.02, q(x_t|x_0) = N(√ᾱ x_0, (1−ᾱ)I) (Ho et al. 2020 eq. 4). Reader scrubs t or replays 1000 sequential steps, which match the closed form (variance 0.98 vs 1.00). At about t = 660 the cloud is as close to N(0, I) as pure noise itself (within 1.1× its own distance).
3. **Arrows that point at the data** (#ch3). Score ∇log p; Langevin x ← x + (η/2)∇log p + √η z. On 0.2·N((−5,−5),I) + 0.8·N((5,5),I) (Song & Ermon 2019 §3.2), plain Langevin from uniform starts ends with 48% in the small mode (wrong weights: the score is ~0 between modes); annealed Langevin, σ 20 → 1 in 10 geometric levels × 100 steps, ε = 0.1 (step η_i = ε σ_i²/σ_L²), gives 23% (truth 20%). Tuned values, not the paper's.
4. **Learning to remove a little noise** (#ch4). ε-objective L_simple (Ho et al. eq. 14); posterior mean = Tweedie (Efron 2011); ε* = −√(1−ᾱ)·score. Figure 1: drag a noisy point, see posterior responsibilities and the posterior mean (= Tweedie, checked). Figure 2: train the same net live in the browser (3000 steps × 128): EMA loss ≈ 0.43; the irreducible loss floor is 0.371 (Monte Carlo, 20,000 draws); the shipped net reaches 0.378.
5. **Walking back** (#ch5). Generalised sampler (DDIM eq. 12, σ from eq. 16): η = 1 is DDPM ancestral sampling, η = 0 is DDIM. Figure of merit #1: quality vs NFE, computed in the page. Net: DDPM needs 100 steps, DDIM 50 to get within 1.5 × floor; at 10 steps DDIM 0.092 vs DDPM 0.137; exact denoiser + DDIM needs 20; net DDIM stalls at ≈ 0.027 (model error). 1 step = the posterior mean of everything; error amplified by 1/√ᾱ_T ≈ 157. Published counterpart: DDIM paper Table 1 (CIFAR-10 FID).
6. **The continuous view: from diffusion to flow** (#ch6). Reverse SDE and probability-flow ODE (Song et al. 2021); flow matching on the linear path x_t = (1−t)z + t·x₁ (Lipman et al. 2023); rectified flow and one reflow step (Liu et al. 2023), with pairs made by RK4 (200 steps) through the exact flow field (20,000 pairs). Paths of 40 points, one-step landing gap (ODE 4.75, FM 0.92, reflow 0.036). Steps needed: DDPM 100, DDIM 50, flow matching 20, reflow 1. Exact FM in one Euler step puts every point at the data mean (checked).
7. **Guidance: diversity for fidelity** (#ch7). Classifier-free guidance ε̃ = (1+w)ε_c − wε_u (Ho & Salimans 2022 eq. 6) on exact class-conditional and unconditional ε, 50 DDIM steps. Figure of merit #2: fidelity (mean Bayes class probability) rises 0.892 → 0.979 (w = 1) → 0.996 (w = 3) while spread falls 0.95 → 0.53 and distance to the class grows 0.019 → 0.471. True samples score 88.6%. Published counterpart: Ho & Salimans Table 1 (ImageNet 64, FID 1.8 → 26.22, IS 53.71 → 260.2).
8. **A perfect denoiser only copies** (#ch8). The minimiser of the DDPM loss on N training points is the empirical-mixture denoiser; sampled (DDIM 250 steps) it reproduces training points: 95% of samples within 0.01 of a training point at N = 16 (true samples 0.4%), 66% at 128 (true 2.3%), median NN 0.010 vs 0.018 at 1024. Nets trained on the same sets: at 128 median 0.049 vs true 0.054 (not copying); at 1024 distance 0.035 vs 0.028 for the infinite-data net. Links to Carlini et al. 2023 and Kadkhodaie et al. 2024.
9. **Latent space and text** (#ch9). Explanation and arithmetic only, and the page says so: an autoencoder with f = 8, c = 4 (Stable Diffusion v1) shrinks 512×512×3 = 786,432 numbers to 64×64×4 = 16,384 (48×); patch 2 → 1,024 tokens vs 65,536 pixel tokens (4,096× fewer attention pairs). Cross-attention diagram. Rombach et al. 2022.
10. **The frontier as of November 2026** (#ch10). Dated, sourced, tagged established / demonstrated / claimed / contested; rendered from a data block in copy (`frontier`, `records`) so it can be updated without touching prose.
11. **Five questions to ask of a news story** (#ch11), summary, links.
