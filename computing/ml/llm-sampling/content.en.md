## What does the model actually output at each step? {#step0 h2=s0t}

::: strings
nav0: 0 Next-word odds
:::

Step 0 · The next-word probabilities {#s0k .step-kicker i18n=text}

This step answers: what does a language model hold in its hands before writing the next word? It first gives every candidate word a score (a **logit**, positive or negative) and then uses softmax to turn the scores into **probabilities** that add up to 1. This page hand-sets the scores of 10 candidate words for each prompt (they are not real model outputs). Switch the prompt and watch the scores (pale bars) turn into probabilities (bright bars): after “The capital of France is” one word takes almost everything, while after “The weather today is” several words are neck and neck. The **entropy** in the readout measures how spread out the distribution is, and the “effective number of options” = 2^entropy is roughly how many words are seriously competing. {#s0e .explain}

::: html step0-1
ctxL: Prompt
cv0: Scores and probabilities of the candidate words
s0reset: Reset prompt
:::

Softmax: p_i = e^(z_i) / Σ e^(z_j); entropy H = −Σ p·log₂p (bits), larger the flatter the distribution. {#s0term .term i18n=text}

## How does temperature sharpen or flatten the distribution? {#step1 h2=s1t}

::: strings
nav1: 1 Temperature
:::

Step 1 · Temperature {#s1k .step-kicker i18n=text}

This step answers: given the same scores, how do we make the model more cautious or more adventurous? The **temperature** T divides every score by T before softmax: p_i ∝ e^(z_i/T). Drag T with the prompt fixed: see how the bright bars change against the dashed outline (the T = 1 distribution). A small T concentrates probability on the top word, and T → 0 means always picking the highest score (greedy); a large T flattens the distribution. The readout gives the top word’s probability, the entropy and the effective number of options. {#s1e .explain}

::: html step1-1
cv1: The probability distribution at different temperatures
s1TL: Temperature T
s1reset: Reset temperature
:::

T < 1 sharpens the distribution, T > 1 flattens it, T = 1 is the model as it is. Temperature changes only the shape; it adds no knowledge. {#s1term .term i18n=text}

s1ro: T = {T}: the most likely word is “<b>{top}</b>” at <b>{p}</b>; entropy <b>{h}</b> bits, effective number of options about <b>{n}</b>.
:::

## How do we cut off the unreliable long tail? {#step2 h2=s2t}

::: strings
nav2: 2 Trimming the tail
:::

Step 2 · Trimming the tail {#s2k .step-kicker i18n=text}

This step answers: besides temperature, how do we avoid occasionally drawing an absurd word? **Top-k** keeps only the k most probable words; **top-p** (nucleus sampling) keeps the fewest words whose cumulative probability just reaches p. Removed words get probability 0 and the rest are renormalised so the total is 1 again. The order on this page: temperature first, then top-k, then top-p. Drag k or p: grey bars are removed words, coloured bars are the renormalised probabilities, and the dashed outline is the probability before trimming; watch “original probability covered” in the readout. Raise T first, then tighten p, to see the tail being cut. {#s2e .explain}

::: html step2-1

s2TL: Temperature T
s2kL: k for top-k
s2pL: p for top-p
s2reset: Reset trimming
:::

Original probability covered = the sum of the kept words’ probabilities before trimming; the smaller, the harsher the cut. {#s2term .term i18n=text}

## How many draws until frequencies look like probabilities? {#step3 h2=s3t}

::: strings
nav3: 3 Sampling
:::

Step 3 · Sampling {#s3k .step-kicker i18n=text}

This step answers: the model draws just one word at a time, so what happens after many draws? The buttons draw at random from the current distribution (the probabilities after temperature T and top-p, the white ticks) and the coloured bars show the frequencies drawn so far. The random seed is fixed, so the same sequence of button presses gives the same result. One draw shows almost no pattern; after 1000 draws the frequencies hug the probabilities. The **total variation distance** TV = ½Σ|frequency − probability| in the readout measures how far apart the two distributions still are (0 = identical, smaller is better). Note: changing T, p or the prompt clears the draws so far, and the page tells you so. {#s3e .explain}

::: html step3-1

s3TL: Temperature T
s3pL: p for top-p
s3d1: Draw 1
s3d100: Draw 100
s3d1000: Draw 1000
s3reset: Clear draws and reset settings
:::

Frequency = times a word was drawn / total draws; TV falls roughly like 1/√N as draws grow (random fluctuation). {#s3term .term i18n=text}

## One word after another: how does a sentence grow? {#step4 h2=s4t}

::: strings
nav4: 4 Word by word
:::

Step 4 · Word by word {#s4k .step-kicker i18n=text}

This step answers: what gets written when “draw one word” is repeated? Below is a hand-set mini model that looks only at the previous word to decide the next word’s probabilities (real models look at the whole preceding text). Press “Next word” or “Draw 12 words”; the sequence grows from “the”, the bars show the next-word probabilities for the current step, and the white frame marks the word just drawn. Try T = 0.1: it nearly always takes the top score and the sentence soon falls into a loop, so the repeat rate rises; at T = 3 the sentence scatters into disorder. The **repeat rate** in the readout = repeated three-word groups as a fraction of all three-word groups in the sequence. “New random seed” clears the current sentence and starts another random path. {#s4e .explain}

## Wrap-up: score → probability → trim → draw one {#step5 h2=s5t}

::: strings
nav5: 5 Finale
:::

Step 5 · Finale {#s5k .step-kicker i18n=text}

A language model does the same thing at every step: score the candidate words, shape the scores with temperature and trimming, then draw one word and append it. {#s5e .explain i18n=text}

::: html step5-1
c1t: Scores to probabilities
c1b: Softmax turns any scores into probabilities that sum to 1; entropy says how many words compete (step 0).
c2t: Temperature shapes
c2b: Small T is more cautious, large T more random; it changes the shape and adds no knowledge (step 1).
c3t: Trim the tail
c3b: Top-k and top-p remove low-probability words and renormalise, at the cost of less variety (step 2).
c4t: Draws accumulate
c4b: One draw is random, many draws approach the probabilities; word by word, low temperature loops (steps 3 and 4).
modelT: Model and limits
modelB: All scores are hand-set and are not outputs of a real model; the mini model in step 4 looks only at the previous word. Real models have vocabularies of tens of thousands of words, read the whole preceding text, and often use other tricks such as repetition penalties. Temperature and trimming only reshape the distribution and do not make an answer more “true”. Sampling uses fixed pseudo-random seeds.
:::
