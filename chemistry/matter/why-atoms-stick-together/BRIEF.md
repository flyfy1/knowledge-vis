## What the page is

One question: why do atoms stick together, and how does the way they stick decide what a thing is like?
11 chapters, 11 models (labs), 12 static diagrams (each a wide and a narrow inline SVG generated by
`tools/diagrams.cjs` from `models.js`; labels come from the copy).

## Decisions

- H₂⁺ rather than H₂ in chapter 2: one electron, so the model on the page is the whole problem and the textbook
  numbers can be reproduced exactly. The page says H₂ needs a second electron and what that adds.
- Chapter 2 shows both the fixed-orbital and optimised-orbital pictures, because they give opposite answers to "is the
  bond a kinetic or a potential effect"; the prose states both and says which is the full account.
- The MD lab is two-dimensional and says so; its temperatures are in ε/k, converted to argon only as a reading.
- Chapter 10 has four lists (established / demonstrated / claimed and disputed / unsolved); each claimed item names
  who claims it and who disputes it.
- Unregistered drafts are mentioned in plain text only: the thermodynamics essay (`which-way-reactions-go`, described,
  not named, in ch5/ch7/ch11), 《芯片为什么越来越难做》 / "Why Faster Chips Keep Getting Harder" (`why-chips-are-hard`,
  ch5 and ch11) and 《电池为什么就是装不下更多电》 / "Why Can't a Battery Just Hold More?" (`why-batteries-hold-so-little`,
  ch11). **For the registrar:** once those pages are live, these mentions can become `[[slug]]` links in both
  languages (and the English titles should be re-checked against their registered titles).

## Tuned, not sourced

- Hückel |β| = 4.62 eV: fitted so that the model returns butadiene's measured 217 nm. The page says so, and shows
  that the same β then gives wrong wavelengths for the other polyenes.
- `TB_BETA_EV = 2` eV for converting the tight-binding gap to eV: a round illustrative value.
- `MD_PHASE` thresholds (gas: mean neighbours < 2.2; solid: |ψ₆| > 0.88 with core fraction > 0.3): chosen by looking
  at runs of this 48-atom system; they label the sentence, not a physical transition temperature. The "melting near
  0.4, boiling near 0.5 ε/k" in the prose is what this small 2-D system does, not argon's phase diagram.
- Thermostat time constant and the animation's steps per frame: chosen for a watchable speed.
- Born exponent n = 8 for NaCl is the conventional textbook choice (average of Ne-like 7 and Ar-like 9).
- Energy ranges in the "forces" ladder are order-of-magnitude textbook ranges.
- Levinthal: 3 states per residue, 10¹³ conformations per second: the customary illustrative values.
