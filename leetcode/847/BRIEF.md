## Terms and causal derivation
Step0: graph=nodes and allowed edges; adjacency list=neighbors by label; walk=sequence of legal moves, length=edge count. Step1: baseline=enumerate walks by length; equivalent histories=same future legal options and completion condition. Two center arrivals1→0 and2→0 have visited{0,1} vs{0,2}; marking only node0 loses necessary histories. Step2 redefines state `(u,mask)` locally; u=current node, mask=integer encoding visited set, bit v weight2^v; `1<<v` makes that weight; OR keeps existing bits and adds v; full=(1<<n)−1. BFS means process all d-move states before d+1; frontier=newly reached pairs in a layer. Example1→0→2→0: masks0010→0011→0111→0111, retaining a revisit with more memory. Step3 repeats local state definitions; step4 repeats code-variable definitions.

Proof: every state in layer d has a legal d-edge witness. One neighbor extends it by1 and OR adds exactly that neighbor. Every legal d+1-edge walk has a d-edge prefix; inductively its pair was reached no later than d, so its successor is reached no later than d+1. Earlier arrival at the same pair dominates later arrival because every future suffix is legal from the same u and covers the same missing nodes. Merge exact pairs only; visited set alone also loses endpoint. Multi-source initialization covers all allowed starts at0. First full-mask layer is globally shortest. Finite ≤n·2^n pairs ensures termination; connectedness supplies a covering walk.
