## Learning loop and five steps
1. **One pig, three buckets.** Known: one poisoned bucket and waiting rule. Question: can one pig tell three buckets apart in 30 minutes? Input: B=3, D=15, T=30; output: three distinguishable observations. Action: read the hand timeline (feed bucket 1, then bucket 2 if alive, never bucket 3), later replay preset. Observe death at 15, death at 30, or survival. Condition: exactly one poisoned bucket; no interrupted wait. Baseline: B-1 pigs each test one bucket in the first round; all survive identifies the reserved last bucket.
2. **Combine outcomes.** Known: one pig has R+1 outcomes. Question: how can two pigs distinguish nine buckets? Input: two rounds, two pigs; output: one distinct pair of outcome digits per bucket. Action: inspect 3x3 hand grid and code meaning. Observe Cartesian pairs, not a sum. Conditions: digit 0 means never feed that bucket to this pig; positive digit is the single feeding round. Derive upper bound and matching construction, including adaptive-strategy bound.
3. **Reversible schedule experiment.** Known: digit schedule. Question: do observations really leave one candidate? Input: presets B=3/9/4(single round)/4(two rounds)/10/1/8(partial wait)/27, selectable hidden bucket; output: actual observations and surviving candidate cards. Action: advance one completed wait, rewind, reset, go to deadline, switch bucket/preset (explicit restart); observe retained history and candidate labels. Conditions: small complete simulations, no omitted rounds. Preset and bucket changes reset this experiment only; quiz state is independent. End at R waits; B=1 has zero pigs, known bucket before testing. Tutorial storage is separate from submission O(1) auxiliary words.
4. **Fresh prediction.** Known: capacity (R+1)^p and its constructive meaning. Question: minimum pigs for 17 buckets with D=10, T=20? Input: fresh scenario; output: choose 2/3/5 then explicitly reveal. Action: predict, reveal, change option; observe feedback clears and explains insufficient 9, sufficient 27, or binary-only overestimate. Conditions: answers remain hidden until selection+reveal, locale preserves choice/reveal.
5. **Implement and check.** Known: least p with capacity >= B. Question: how does the proof become Python? Input: official arguments; output: integer p. Action: read/copy/download exact source, inspect capacity ledger from experiment and map multiplication to loop. Conditions: valid domain ensures states>=2; B=1 returns 0; floor complete rounds; exact integer loop avoids floating log boundary errors. Costs: O(1+log_(R+1) B) arithmetic time, O(1) auxiliary machine words under official bounds. Browser snapshot costs are disclosed separately.
