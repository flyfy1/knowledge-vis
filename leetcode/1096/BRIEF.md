## Derivation/proof and costs
At one brace level, all scanned alternatives before the latest comma are exactly U; words in the current unfinished concatenation are exactly T. Empty product {""} is necessary since ""+a=a. A letter or recursive child supplies atom; all pair joins T×atom construct exactly every legal continuation and no others. Comma commits T to U and resets T; stopping at this level's closing brace/end returns U∪T. Child has its own U/T; shared i advances, parent local sets stay unchanged until returned atom is multiplied. Closing brace consumed by caller; no mutable-set backtracking undo. Grammar nesting makes induction valid; each read consumes a token or closes a call, so terminates.

Baseline replaces first innermost {...}, branches once per comma alternative, repeats until no braces, then deduplicates and sorts. Branches duplicate full work on {a,a}{b,c}; canonical sets discard equality before later products. Let n=input length, B=number of terminal baseline derivations including duplicates, K=distinct final words, W>=1=largest set size anywhere in canonical parser including identity/intermediate sets. Baseline upper bound O(n²B+nK log K) expected time and O(n²+nK) space with depth-first implementation; copying strings and storing final words counted. Canonical upper bound O(n²W²+nK log K) expected time; O(n²W) auxiliary/output space. At most n product steps, each ≤W² concatenations of length≤n, and ≤n active local levels each retaining O(W) strings of length≤n. Hashing expected; sorting comparisons O(n). Conservative bounds, not a tight linear claim. {a,b} repeated t times has2^t distinct words, so output growth unavoidable. Single letter n1,W1 and brace-free60-letter string remain covered. Teaching snapshots retain all frames and copies; not submission memory.
