## Source, correctness and cost audit

The actual Python allocates cash/hold of length k+1, copies each before a day and loops t=1..k. h[t] reads old_cash[t−1]−price; c[t] reads old_hold[t]+price. The JS simulator uses the same dependencies, plus independent history storage. In the observed day-3 visual transition, h[2] has old −2, candidate 3−1=2 and completed 2. This is exactly Python `hold[2] = max(old_hold[2], old_cash[1] - price)` with old_cash[1]=3. Selling at day 4 reads that prior holding cash, not freshly updated cash.

Invariant: after a prefix, cash[t] maximizes receipts minus payments among flat histories with at most t sales; hold[t] does so among holding histories with at most t buys. Holding entails one more buy than sale. Each candidate extends an eligible prior history, and every legal final action is either rest or the specified buy/sell. Zero initial cash permits unused budgets; −∞ excludes initially holding a share. Returning cash[k] excludes unfinished purchases. Same-day sell/buy can be removed at equal price without hurting profit under these rules. This justifies the strict previous-day simulation against the official permissions.

The hand example precedes the abstract arrays; the later tuple walkthrough precedes the generic recurrence. The baseline's repeated futures, dominance argument and rest-versus-action cases explain the saved work. The baseline exact-count histories to at-most budget aggregation paragraph is dense and needed a second reading, but its cash example, explicit aggregation sentence and buying-from-t−1 rule supply the bridge without author notes. The transfer exercise distinguishes owning a share from merely having flat cash. The small-dips example exposes a tempting wrong greedy rule; it is a substantive counterexample, not only a heading.

Actual cost is O(nk) time for official n≥1, including O(k) copies per day, and O(k) auxiliary memory for two current and two old arrays; inputs are not mutated. Baseline branches at most twice each day, hence O(2^n) time/O(n) stack; it is illustrative and infeasible at full bounds. Simulation retains O(nk) history separately. No claimed unlimited-budget shortcut exists in the submission. Python infinity sentinels cannot become a returned impossible state because all flat states initially admit doing nothing.
