## What the review establishes about Master and minimax

The archived canonical source implements
`Solution.findSecretWord(words: List[str], master: 'Master') -> None`.
The judge supplies `Master.guess`; the function does not return the secret. The
reported official contract uses unique six-letter words, a secret from that list,
positional-match replies (6 means success; an illegal word yields −1), and an
allowed-call limit in 10–30. That limit is not a parameter of this Python interface.
All canonical probes are taken from the supplied list. A singleton candidate still
requires a successful actual call; merely deducing it does not satisfy the goal.

The source explicitly caps itself at **10 calls**, including on tasks whose allowed
budget is larger, and labels its selection strategy a heuristic. It caches pair
scores, considers unused probes, minimizes the largest **unsuccessful** reply bucket
0–5, prefers a current candidate on ties, then original order. Reply 6 returns
immediately; otherwise exact-equality filtering retains only candidates matching
the new reply. The visual's seven columns and code's six unsuccessful buckets are
different presentations of this same success/continuation split.

The reviewer reconstructed truthful-history filtering correctness and strict
progress **without the ten-call cap**: the true secret is retained; inconsistent
words are excluded; an unused candidate probe offers an unsuccessful bucket of
size at most `c−1`. This supports an uncapped bound of at most `n` calls. It does
**not** prove a universal ten-call bound or success of this heuristic on every
generated judge case. The source can exhaust its ten iterations and return without
a reply of 6. The report explicitly retains a 26 repeated-letter-word stress case
with ten unsuccessful calls as a limitation demonstration. A generated-case
qualification is not a proof for this particular heuristic, and no successful
online submission is claimed.

Observed evidence includes a fresh independently predicted two-call example,
