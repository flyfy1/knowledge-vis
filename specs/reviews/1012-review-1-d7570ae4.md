`perm` computes falling products, including returning 1 for k=0. Shorter lengths prohibit leading zero; equal-length smaller candidates start at 1 only in position zero. Their suffix count has 10−(i+1)=9−i available digits and D−i−1 positions. The equal path stops at the first repetition; otherwise the final +1 includes n. These are disjoint by length or first differing position/digit, so the complement neither misses nor double-counts numbers. A digit strictly smaller than the first bound digit frees every subsequent legal position; comparing each later digit independently with n would be wrong.
