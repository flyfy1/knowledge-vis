The invariant is an equivalence of reachability before/after each forced batch while both coordinates exceed their starts. The unchanged coordinate above its start excludes an early encounter with the start inside that batch. A batch can end at zero, but that is explicitly labeled a failed arithmetic state, not a legal positive point. The positive divisors in the loop cannot become zero before the loop exits. Each update decreases the positive-state sum; finite termination follows. Once a coordinate matches its start, monotonicity forbids changing it, giving exactly the nonnegative divisible difference test. These facts also cover smaller-target and equality failures.

The browser's first (3,17) batch yields (3,2), records `17 = 5 × 3 + 2`, and leaves x unchanged. This maps to `ty %= tx`; the history count is completed operations, not forward move count. Final frames leave coordinates unchanged and append a check, so frame counts, point values, equations and verdicts agree. Rewind restores prior histories without recomputing a contradictory final value. Source rebinds local immutable integers only. Cost is O(1+log M) time for fixed-range arithmetic: after at most two nonterminal remainder updates, the maximum halves; the boundary return is constant work. O(1) auxiliary space matches the actual solution. Baseline BFS has at most tx·ty visited grid points, including a shared-coordinate boundary; its upper bound is valid.
