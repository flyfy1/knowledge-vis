### Correctness, cost and translation observations

The parser's U contains exactly the completed comma alternatives at the current level. T contains all and only concatenations of completed atoms in the current alternative. The empty word is needed as the initial product identity, whereas U starts empty. Each comma adds T to U before replacing T with the identity. A recursive call owns new local sets; the parent's sets remain live, not mutated or reconstructed by undo. Each returned group is an atom and every pairing adds exactly one legal continuation. On valid input the cursor progresses over letters/delimiters and returns at the correct close/end, so the outer union is complete and sorted only once. This matches both the rendered trace and actual source. The one-character, no-brace, nested and duplicate checks cover these different paths.

The baseline really branches over textual substitutions and delays duplicate elimination. In `{a,a}{b,c}`, early set elimination reduces later pairings from four duplicate derivations to two distinct results. The lesson explicitly limits this benefit when all words differ. Its conservative expected-time `O(n²W²+nK log K)` and space `O(n²W)` are valid: at most n products, at most W² pairs per product, O(n) string construction/hashing per pair; at most n simultaneously live levels holding O(W) strings of length at most n; final comparisons cost at most n characters. W includes the empty-word seed and is at least one; brace-free n=60 and n=1 do not zero out required work. Baseline `O(n²B+nK log K)` time and `O(n²+nK)` space cover at most n recursion levels per terminal derivation and retained strings/results. These are loose upper bounds, not timing claims. Snapshot storage is explicitly excluded from submission memory.
