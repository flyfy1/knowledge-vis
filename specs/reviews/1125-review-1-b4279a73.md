## Independent calculations, invariants and code transfer

The independently chosen four-skill input returned [1,2]. Independent enumeration with combinations and set union found that it is the only minimum team. No mutation of req_skills or people occurred. Saved in `independent.json`. This is a code check, not a custom-input browser experiment.

The temptation to retain only one size-1 team fails here: [0] covers a,b while [1] covers a,c. After person 2 (b,d), only the latter completes coverage in two people. A greedy tie that picks 0 then 1 still needs a third person for d. The exclusion is based on different future coverage, not an arbitrary index preference.

For each prefix the invariant is exact coverage plus minimum team length. Initially 0 maps to [] and all other masks are absent. For a next-prefix team containing i, removing i yields a prior-prefix coverage; replacing that remainder by its shortest stored representative preserves the union after adding i, without increasing size. Omitting i leaves an old state. All constructed candidates are legal and have distinct processed indices. This proves both the lower bound and an attaining construction. Termination is after n whole-person rounds; feasibility makes dp[full] defined.

The hand trace's source mask 001, team [0], new mask 110 produces merged=111; absent destination stores [0,2]. The later source 011 constructs size 3 and loses to size 2 already stored in that round. Thus card “before round” and comparison “stored before comparison” correctly describe different instants. No final candidate identity is incorrectly paired with the winning value. `list(dp.items())` freezes the source entries, not deep copies; `team + [i]` allocates new lists, so referenced old teams remain unchanged. Python dictionary insertion order can break ties differently from numeric JS object-key order; any optimum is permitted, and the lesson does not require a unique team.

Actual cost reasoning: L skill entries build person masks; each of n people scans at most 2^m prior states. Accepted lists have at most m indices, because every person in a minimum team must supply some unique skill. Hence O(L+n·m·2^m) time and O(m·2^m+m) auxiliary space (including the snapshot and old lists retained during a round). With official m≥1, even L=0 for individual empty-skilled people leaves round work covered by n·m·2^m. Input reading, output and empty masks are accounted for. The baseline really enumerates combinations, unions up to n·m entries per team and uses O(n+m) auxiliary space. The teaching history's O(n·m·2^m) storage is explicitly separate.

Boundary evidence covers empty-skilled people, duplicates/ties, one required skill, all skills in one person, a later one-person improvement, 16 distinct single-skill contributors plus 44 empty people, 59 empty people followed by the sole sufficient person, and 16-character skill names.
