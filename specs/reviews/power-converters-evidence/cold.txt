step0 STEP 0 / 6 · Voltage & power

Twice the voltage does not mean twice the energy

Voltage is energy per charge (1 V = 1 J/C). Current is charge per second (1 A = 1 C/s). Their product is energy transferred each second: power.

P = V · I　　Ideal steady state: Pin = Pout

With a fixed 12 W power budget, move the output from 6 V to 24 V and watch output current:

Output voltage 6 V
Output power
12 W
Output current
2.00 A
Relation
I = 12 / V

This is a power budget, not yet a circuit simulation. A converter uses storage components and switches to redirect energy at different times.

Two conservation rules for reading circuits

Arrows show conventional current: the direction positive charge would move. Currents entering a node sum to currents leaving it (Kirchhoff’s current law, KCL). Signed voltage rises and drops around a closed loop sum to zero (Kirchhoff’s voltage law, KVL).

Node: iL = Iout + iC
Loop example: Vin − vL − Vout = 0

step1 STEP 1 / 6 · Inductor

Inductor: current cannot jump without cause

An inductor stores energy in a magnetic field. Voltage sets how quickly current changes, not the current itself.

v = L · di/dt → Δi = v · Δt / L
E = ½Li²

Try two positive pulses, a zero pulse, then a negative pulse. Does the curve instantly return to zero?

L = 10 mH · Δt = 0.5 ms · One interval per click; up to 16 intervals.

step2 STEP 2 / 6 · Capacitor

Capacitor: give voltage a memory

A capacitor stores energy in an electric field. Current entering its positive terminal adds charge and raises its voltage; current leaving lowers it.

i = C · dv/dt → Δv = i · Δt / C
E = ½Cv²

Try two positive pulses, a zero pulse, then a negative pulse. Does the curve instantly return to zero?

step3 STEP 3 / 6 · Switches & timing

Switches choose the path; timing sets the ratio

An ideal closed switch acts as a wire; an open switch blocks current. An ideal diode allows current from anode A toward cathode K, with zero forward drop and no reverse current. This is a teaching approximation.

Reverse diode

5 V → K |◁ A → 1 kΩ → return: blocked, I = 0 mA

This separate DC experiment has a 5 V source, a 1 kΩ resistor and a series diode, with a return wire to the negative source terminal.

Pulse-width modulation (PWM) repeats switching every period T. Duty D is ON time divided by period; frequency f = 1/T. This lesson represents 20,000 cycles per second, but you can step through them slowly.

How does a real supply hold its output steady?

We manually selected duty above: open-loop operation. Real regulators usually measure output, compare it with a target and adjust duty: this is feedback control . This lesson does not simulate a controller or startup.

As D approaches 1, the ideal boost formula diverges. Real circuits are limited by current, magnetic saturation, losses and voltage ratings. This lesson restricts parameters to positive inductor current. Light loads may enter discontinuous conduction, requiring a different analysis.

真实电源如何保持输出稳定？

前面手动选择占空比，这是开环。实际电源通常测量输出、与目标比较，再调整占空比，这就是反馈控制。本页没有模拟控制器或启动过程。

D 趋近 1 时，理想 Boost 公式趋于无穷大，但真实电路会受电流限制、线圈饱和、损耗及元件耐压约束。本课限定参数使电感电流始终为正；轻载时可能进入断续导通，公式要重新分析。
