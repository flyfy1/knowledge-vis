# #1036 Escape a Large Maze

Dispatcher-only reference to the [official problem](https://leetcode.com/problems/escape-a-large-maze/).
Ordinary public GraphQL returned HTTP200, Hard/free, matching the fixed catalog.
Raw response, full statement and language templates are preserved beside this note.

The original board has **10^6 by 10^6 cells**, coordinates0 through999999 on each
axis. Input is `blocked`, `source`, `target`; there is no board-size argument.
Up to200 cells are blocked. Endpoints are distinct and neither is blocked. Moves
are one orthogonal cell, never diagonal or outside the board. Return a Boolean
reachability result, not a shortest route/distance.

Python3: `Solution.isEscapePossible(self, blocked: list[list[int]], source:
list[int], target: list[int]) -> bool`.
TypeScript: `isEscapePossible(blocked: number[][], source: number[], target:
number[]): boolean`.

## Direct distinguishing examples

- Official corner trap: blockers`[[0,1],[1,0]]`, source`[0,0]`, target`[0,2]`
  is false. The two remaining directions leave the real board.
- Swap the endpoints: the target is trapped even though the source has open
  space. Reaching open space from just one end does not certify a connection.
- With the same blockers, `[0,0]` cannot reach`[1,1]`: diagonal adjacency is not
  a legal move.
- With only blocker`[0,1]`, `[0,0]` reaches`[0,2]` via`[1,0]→[1,2]→[0,2]`, where
  each arrow expands into orthogonal unit moves.
- A two-cell pocket`{[2,2],[2,3]}` is enclosed by
  `[[1,2],[2,1],[3,2],[1,3],[3,3],[2,4]]`. It cannot reach`[0,0]`, but its two
  cells can reach each other. “Inside an enclosure” alone does not imply false.

Nine checked cases include both official examples and the upper-right real
boundary. Positive witnesses are axis-aligned segments with all cells inside the
million-grid and no blocker on a segment. Negative witnesses enumerate every
exit edge of a tiny closed cell set and verify a blocker or the actual board edge.
The empty-board official case uses two symbolic straight segments, not a
million-cell allocation. No arbitrary cropped-grid failure is used as evidence.

## Provisional learning/review risks

Distinguish a visualization viewport from the true board. A route leaving a crop
may still exist; its absence from the picture is not a blocked boundary. Clarify
both endpoint conditions, the asymmetry of a local search observation and the
symmetry of actual reachability. If bounded exploration is chosen later, its
finite stopping bound and endpoint reasoning need their own proof; these notes
provide no enclosure-size theorem or chosen search algorithm. Reaching the target
within an enclosure must remain a success. Preserve original coordinate values
instead of silently shrinking/replacing the official input.

Large coordinates and sparse blockers make full-board enumeration unsuitable as
a default teaching trace. No full search, worst-case performance or canonical
implementation is tested here. Node/edge counts, storage and any integer-key
representation need review against the actual implementation and language.
