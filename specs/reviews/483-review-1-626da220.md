Reconstructed the proof independently:

- Every representation has a highest exponent m≥1. Base≥2 implies at most L binary digits, so trying m=L−1 down to 2, then the two-digit fallback, covers all possibilities.
- For different successful lengths a>b, a base x≥y would make Sₐ(x)>Sᵦ(y); therefore a longer representation must use a smaller base. This is why descending m can return immediately.
- For fixed m, Sₘ(k) strictly increases. The initial lower bound is 2; kᵐ≤N<2ᴸ implies k<2^(L/m), so the rounded-up power-of-two upper bound is safe, even though loose.
- After j multiply-adds, `total` equals 1+k+…+kʲ. Once it exceeds N, omitted terms are positive; a high comparison is valid even without completing all m iterations. Equality cannot occur spuriously after an early stop because stopping requires strictly greater than N.
- Each low/high comparison excludes the midpoint and the entire proved-impossible side; `lo=k+1` or `hi=k−1` strictly decreases an inclusive interval. Empty intervals terminate a length, and equality uses all m multiply-adds. No excluded candidate can be a solution.
- The fallback N−1 always works as two ones. All longer lengths were exhausted first, so it too is minimal when reached.
