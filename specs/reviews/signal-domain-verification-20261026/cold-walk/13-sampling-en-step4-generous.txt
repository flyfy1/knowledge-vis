Step 4 · The frequency view

Aliasing in the frequency domain: the spectrum clones itself

On the Fourier page we saw that a pure tone is a single peak in the spectrum. Sampling has a side effect: the whole spectrum is copied endlessly, one copy every fs. The same story on the z-plane: strips and twin points → With a generous fs the copies ignore each other and the middle slice [0, fs/2] is enough to rebuild the signal; with a stingy one, a peak from a neighbouring copy lands inside that slice — that is the fake wave from step 2, with its frequency-domain birth certificate. Drag fs and watch the copy spacing change.

Signal frequency f (Hz) 13

Copy spacing = f_s = 30 Hz · f_s > 2f: the baseband [0, 15.0 Hz] holds only the original peak. Clean.

Notice what aliasing really is: not “the signal got a bit dirty”, but high-frequency information moved bodily into the low frequencies, impersonating them. Once sampling is done, the true peak and the alias peak look identical — no filter afterwards can separate them. Prevention has to happen before sampling.
