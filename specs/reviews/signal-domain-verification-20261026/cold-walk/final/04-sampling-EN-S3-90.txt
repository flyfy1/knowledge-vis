Step 3 · The line

The Nyquist line: twice, and not a hair less

This step compares ideal single tones. The Nyquist condition assumes a known bandlimit: if all components satisfy |f| ≤ B and fₛ > 2B, the complete uniform sample sequence determines the signal within that band. Here B=f: sweep fₛ across 2f. Without restricting candidate frequencies, higher aliases can still share every sample. Straight joins are display interpolation; their varying height does not decide exact reconstruction. Pin fₛ=2f and change phase to inspect the boundary ambiguity.

Signal frequency f (Hz) 4
Sample rate f_s (Hz) 8.0
Sampling phase φ (°) 90
Pin to exactly 2f

f_s = 2f = 8.0 Hz: all down to phase — max |sample| = 1.00 (1 = full amplitude, 0 = wiped out).

At the boundary x[n] = A(−1)ⁿ sinφ. With φ=90° the dots alternate at full amplitude; with φ=0° they vanish. Even nonzero dots determine only A sinφ, not A and φ separately: A=1, φ=30° and A=0.5, φ=90° give identical samples. Nonzero dots are no guarantee of recovery.
