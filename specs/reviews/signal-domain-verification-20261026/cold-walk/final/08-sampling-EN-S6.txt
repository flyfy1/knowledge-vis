Before you sample, learn to squint

Anti-aliasing suppresses high frequencies before decimation. Both sides use the same source and decimator; the right side first applies a finite box convolution twice. Compare how much moiré weakens and how much detail is lost. A box filter has sidelobes and cannot guarantee zero aliasing; results depend on the pattern and stride. Audio also needs anti-alias filtering before analog-to-digital conversion. For a 20 kHz passband and 44.1 kHz sample rate, the Nyquist frequency is 22.05 kHz, leaving a 2.05 kHz transition region for a practical filter to attenuate; it does not cut abruptly at 20 kHz. This illustrates a bandwidth budget, not the historical derivation of 44.1 kHz. Stopband attenuation, hearing limits and hardware performance need separate specifications.

The sampling theorem does not really say “sample as fast as you can”. It says “decide what to give up first, then sample”: you surrender the high-frequency detail precisely so it cannot dress up as low frequency and lie to you.
