The fresh input's core frames independently matched the saved prediction exactly: []→[3]→[2]→[2,5]→[2,4]→[2,4,6]. This is a **code check**, not custom browser input. In the fourth update, [2,5] with height4 probes index1/value5 then index0/value2, returns p=1 and replaces 5 with4. In Python this is `pos=bisect_left(tails, height)` followed by `tails[pos]=height`. The browser's replacement preset fourth step likewise maps [3,5,6] and height2 to p=0 and [2,5,6]. Old values in probe records are genuinely pre-update values; persisted histories are copies, not aliases of the current tails.

Invariant reconstruction: after each prefix, tails[k-1] is the minimum attainable final height of a strict subsequence of length k. Removing the last item from a minimum length-(k+1) witness proves its preceding height, and hence the minimum length-k ending, is smaller. A first->= index p means a length-p witness can extend with h, but no length-(p+1) witness can extend. Shorter entries are already smaller than h and longer lengths cannot newly end with h. Append extends maximum length; replacement preserves attainable lengths and improves one minimum. Descending tied heights excludes equal-width double selection; equal heights replace rather than extend. Empty prefix, singleton, equal dimensions, final termination after n items and maximal increasing/decreasing inputs agree. This supplies the correctness bridge independently of numerical test totals.

Cost audit: sorting and n binary searches give O(n log n) time. `sorted` creates a shallow list of references, sorting workspace plus tails use O(n) auxiliary space; inner lists are never modified. Amortized append is O(1), replacement O(1). The educational simulator eagerly stores entire prefix histories and is substantially larger; the lesson explicitly excludes its snapshot cost from submission complexity. Baseline O(n²) and O(n) are correct.
