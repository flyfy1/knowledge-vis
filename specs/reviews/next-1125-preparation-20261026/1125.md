# #1125 Smallest Sufficient Team: contract and tiny semantic evidence

Official source: https://leetcode.com/problems/smallest-sufficient-team/
The archived ordinary public GraphQL response is HTTP 200, Hard, free, and
contains the full statement, examples, metadata and available language templates.

## Callable and constraints

Python3: `Solution.smallestSufficientTeam(self, req_skills: list[str],
people: list[list[str]]) -> list[int]`.
TypeScript: `smallestSufficientTeam(req_skills: string[], people: string[][]): number[]`.
The return value identifies people by their original zero-based input indices.
Any sufficient team with minimum cardinality is valid, in any output order.
There is no lexicographic, lowest-index, cheapest-person or unique-answer rule.

There are 1..16 distinct required skills and 1..60 people. Skill names contain
1..16 lowercase English letters. Each person's list has 0..16 distinct skills,
and every listed skill belongs to the required set. A sufficient team is
guaranteed. Empty skills lists are legal; unrelated/out-of-requirements skills
are not valid inputs. A person may also add no new coverage relative to an
already selected team. Different people may have identical skill lists.

## Independent subset fixtures

`check-examples.py` directly enumerates combinations of original person indices,
unions their skill sets, and collects every sufficient subset at the first
successful size. This oracle uses no mask DP, transition cache or predecessor
reconstruction. Fixtures contain at most six people and six required skills;
this exhaustive approach is only a tiny reference, not a proposed full-constraint
solution. Eight fixtures include both official examples.

- With people `[[], [a], [], [b]]`, the minimum team is `[1, 3]`. Removing empty
  entries must not renumber people in the answer.
- With `[[a,b], [a], [a,b], []]`, both `[0]` and `[2]` are valid minima. A validator
  demanding just one reference output would reject a correct answer.
- With `[[a], [b], [a], [b]]`, four distinct two-person teams are optimal. Each
  reversed output order is accepted by the fixture validator.
- With `[[a], [b], [a,b]]`, the later person alone is smaller than the earlier
  sufficient pair. Coverage alone does not establish minimality.
- With `[[a,b], [b,c]]`, overlapping skill b remains covered by their union.
  Reusing one person cannot add any new skills. A team is a set of people;
  duplicate indices are rejected, and a larger sufficient team is rejected.

## Reproduced isolated transition and reconstruction failures

These probes deliberately execute incorrect snippets; they are not findings
against an existing lesson or author implementation.

1. For masks `011` and `110`, OR gives `111`, but XOR gives `101`, incorrectly
   removing their shared skill. The direct set-union oracle keeps that skill.
2. Assigning a predecessor's mutable list to a new team, then appending a person,
   changes the prior state's empty team too: the probe leaves both state 0 and
   state 1 pointing to `[0]`. A coverage-state label and its reconstructed team
   therefore disagree. This is a concrete alias failure, not a claim that every
   in-place DP update is invalid.
3. Filtering empty lists and returning the filtered oracle's `[0,1]` fails when
   interpreted against the original people list; `[1,3]` is the actual minimum.

A mask can represent the union of required skills, not how often a skill was
seen. The experiment confirms `mask | mask == mask`. That alone neither enforces
unique returned indices nor proves an arbitrary predecessor chain valid.
No general prohibition of in-place updates, chosen DP architecture, optimized
implementation, runtime benchmark, UI design or submission acceptance is inferred.
