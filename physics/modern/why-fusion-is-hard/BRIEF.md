## The one question

为什么核聚变总是“还要三十年”？The essay answers by climbing: where the energy is (masses), why it is hard to get at (a Coulomb wall, crossed only by tunnelling in the Maxwellian tail), what "working" means (power balance → the Lawson triple product), the two ways to hold the fuel (magnetic, inertial), why plasma gain is not plant gain, the three engineering walls behind ignition (tritium, neutrons, exhaust), where the records stand in 2026, and finally where the phrase itself comes from. The last chapter gives both readings: the 1976 plan tied dates to budgets that were never paid, and the physics also turned out harder than the 1970s plans assumed.

## Finishing pass, 2026-11-18

### The SPARC Q question (HANDOFF open issue a)

Decision: the model was not wrong and nothing was tuned to reach 11. The gap is the flat-profile assumption, and the page now shows that with the model itself.

- Read Creely et al. 2020 in full (§4, tables 1–2). Their Q ≈ 11 is a 0-D POPCON with the same ITER98(y,2) scaling at H98 = 1, the same Q definition (P_fus / (P_RF + P_ohmic) = 140 / 12.8), and P_loss excluding radiation. What differs from the page is the profile and impurity set: T peaking ν_T = 2.5, density peaking ν_ni = 1.33 (ν_ne ≈ 1.43), main-ion dilution 0.85, Z_eff 1.5, radiation ≈ 2.25 × bremsstrahlung.
- The preset was slightly off the published point (f_G 0.35, 7 keV, κ 1.7). It now uses table 2: f_G 0.37 (⟨n_e⟩ 3.1e20), ⟨T⟩ 7.3 keV, κ_a 1.75 (V = 20.8 m³). Flat profiles then give 69 MW, Q 3.3, β_N 1.00 (paper β_N 1.0).
- `tokamak({ peaked: true })` adds parabolic-power profiles x0(1−ρ²)^(ν−1) with those four published numbers. Result: 152 MW (paper 140), Q 8.2 (paper 11), τ_E 0.60 s (paper 0.77 s). The fusion power matches; the remaining Q gap is the denominator, P_aux = P_loss + P_rad − P_α, a small difference of large terms with P_loss ∝ W^3.2. The paper's β (0.012) corresponds to ⟨n⟩⟨T⟩ without the ⟨nT⟩ profile factor 1.18 that this model includes; that alone moves Q by more than a factor of two. The page says so (ch6.p5) and the test pins the sensitivity.
- Consequence stated on the page (ch6.p6): the flat model's agreement with ITER (521 MW, Q 10.4) is partly coincidence. The same four assumptions applied to ITER give 928 MW, Q 27; they are SPARC's assumptions, not ITER's.
- Not reproduced, quoted as the paper's own claims: Q ≈ 4 with a flat density profile, Q > 2 at H98 = 0.7 (the paper re-optimises the operating point; at fixed ⟨n⟩, ⟨T⟩ this model gives Q 1.5 at H = 0.7).
