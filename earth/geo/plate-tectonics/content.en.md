Step 0 · Map first {#s0k .step-kicker i18n=text}

The lithosphere is a set of rigid plates floating on a slowly moving asthenosphere. Plates travel only centimetres per year, yet open oceans and raise mountains over millions of years. Drag time to see one boundary accumulate consequences. {#s0e .explain}

Step 1 · Three boundaries {#s1k .step-kicker i18n=text}

Choose a boundary, then change plate speed. Divergence makes new crust, convergence consumes old crust, and transform motion locks stress on either side of a fault. Arrows show relative motion; colours show common surface outcomes. {#s1e .explain}

## Slow does not mean powerless {#step2 h2=s2t}

::: strings
nav2: 2 Speed & heat
:::

Step 2 · Speed & heat {#s2k .step-kicker i18n=text}

Plate speed sets how much area moves per unit time; mantle heat sets how easily rock deforms. Together they change three observables: seafloor spreading, stress buildup and volcanic activity. Predict first, then drag to test. {#s2e .explain}

::: html step2-1
speed: Plate speed
heat: Mantle heat
spread: Seafloor spread
stress: Stress buildup
volcano: Volcanic activity
:::

These are relative teaching quantities: the model puts “speed × time” and “heat → deformability” on one ledger to expose causal direction, not make a real forecast. {#s2h .hint i18n=text}
