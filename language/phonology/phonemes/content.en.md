## Change one feature and meaning changes track {#step1 h2=s1t}

::: strings
nav1: 1 Contrast

Step 1 · Phonemic Contrast {#s1k .step-kicker i18n=text}

Two words that differ by one sound yet have different meanings form a **minimal pair**. Switch pairs and identify the changing feature; the line below maps a sound difference to a meaning difference. {#s1e .explain}

::: html step1-1
:::

A phoneme is not one recording’s “sound particle”; it is an **abstract category that can distinguish meaning** in a language. The voicing contrast /p/–/b/ does that in pat/bat. {#s1hint .hint}

## A syllable is not a blur: it has slots {#step2 h2=s2t}

::: strings
nav2: 2 Syllable Frame

Step 2 · Syllable Frame {#s2k .step-kicker i18n=text}

Choose a word, then click a segment. A typical syllable has **onset**, **nucleus**, and **coda**: the nucleus is the required peak; the other slots can be empty. {#s2e .explain}

::: html step2-1
onset: Onset
nucleus: Nucleus

onsetLegend: Onset: consonants before the peak

:::

A syllable is structure, not just duration. In splash, /spl/ sits before /æ/ and forms the onset; /ʃ/ sits after it as the coda. Click a segment to keep structure and explanation in sync. {#s2hint .hint}

## A rule sends one suffix to different surface positions {#step3 h2=s3t}

::: strings
nav3: 3 Context
:::

Step 3 · Contextual Variant {#s3k .step-kicker i18n=text}

Choose the root-final sound and watch English plural suffix **assimilation**. The underlying suffix can be written /s/ or /z/; after a voiced sound, voicing spreads and it surfaces as [z], while after a voiceless sound it surfaces as [s]. {#s3e .explain}

::: html step3-1
rootLabel: Root-final sound

root_dog: dog /g/ (voiced)
suffixLabel: Underlying plural suffix
suffix_s: /s/
suffix_z: /z/
replay: ▶ Replay the rule
:::

Square brackets [ ] record the **surface sound**; slashes / / record a phonemic category. The rule does not create a new meaning—it makes adjacent sounds easier to pronounce together. {#s3hint .hint}
